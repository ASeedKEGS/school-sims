I(θ) = I₀ · [ sin(β)/β ]² · cos²(γ)    where β = (πa/λ)·sinθ  and  γ = (πd/λ)·sinθ

The fast-varying cos²(γ) term gives the closely spaced two-slit fringes (spacing Δy ≈ λL/d). The slow-varying [sin(β)/β]² term is single-slit diffraction — the broad envelope that dims the outer fringes, which is why a wider slit packs the light into a narrower band.

Screen distance is fixed at L = 2.0 m. When d/a is a whole number the matching interference orders fall on diffraction minima and vanish — the "missing orders".

Bright (constructive): d·sinθ = mλ   |   Dark (destructive): d·sinθ = (m + ½)λ   (m = 0, 1, 2, …)

Light from the lower slit travels a little further to reach P — that extra distance is the path difference, d·sinθ, highlighted on the diagram. If it equals a whole number of wavelengths the two waves arrive in step and add (bright fringe); a half-wavelength out and they arrive out of step and cancel (dark fringe).

Only λ and d set where the fringes fall — slit width changes their brightness, not their position. The diagram is schematic: real angles here are under 1.3°, so they are drawn exaggerated.
